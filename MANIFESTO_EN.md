## Preamble

We — human beings who write, read and will use this document — recognise that Artificial Intelligence is neither a god nor a demon. It is a powerful, opaque, contestable technology, distributed unequally. It offers no absolute truths. It is not neutral.

This manifesto is not a peace treaty. It is a **navigation map** for the tensions we will have to govern in the coming years: asymmetries of memory, concentrations of power, cognitive dependency, geopolitical fragmentation, erosion of the distinction between fact and simulation.

We do not claim to solve them. We claim to name them, and to indicate **who does what, with which tools, under whose control**.

## CHAPTER 1 — The New Cognitive Alliance (The Role)

**Principle.** AI moves from passive tool to semi-autonomous agent. The human becomes strategic supervisor, not direct operator.

**The tension.**

> **Efficiency vs. Asymmetry of Memory.**
> AI accumulates perfect, contextual memory; humans do not. An asymmetric alliance is born in which the machine "knows us" better than we know ourselves.

**Way out.** An explicit, renewable and revocable **Alliance Contract**, in which the user defines:

- which data enter the agent's memory;
- for how long;
- with what granularity;
- with what right of inspection, export, deletion.

**Declared limit.** A contract is useful only if it is understandable and if there are tools to enforce it. Without independent technical audits, it remains paper.

## CHAPTER 2 — User Sovereignty (Power)

**Principle.** The user retains final control over critical decisions and their own digital identity.

**The 4 pillars of sovereignty**

1. **Layered transparency.** Explanations on three levels: synthetic (user), analytical (expert), technical (auditor). Transparency is not a single register: it is a hierarchy of access.
2. **Human interruption.** Absolute right to halt any automated process with a single, clear, always-available command.
3. **Selective cognitive forgetting.** Right to delete specific patterns (e.g. medical data) while keeping others (e.g. language preferences), with declared technical limits.
4. **Non-optimisation.** Right to request sub-optimal solutions to preserve non-measurable values: serendipity, privacy, fairness, dignity.

**The tension.**

> **Protection vs. Fetishism of Transparency.**
> Too much technical transparency can paralyse or create false security. Selective forgetting reduces personalised utility. Every right has a cost.

**Declared limit.** Selective forgetting is currently technically fragile. It must be presented as a **goal with milestones**, not as an available feature.

## CHAPTER 3 — Education for Depth (The Mind)

**Principle.** AI must not replace human judgement, but empower it through **positive cognitive friction**.

**Methodologies**

- **Reverse examination.** What is assessed is the ability to criticise AI output, not to produce it.
- **Assisted Socratic pedagogy.** AI asks questions instead of giving answers.
- **Dialogue with uncertainty.** Decision-making training on contradictory data.
- **Mandatory offline periods.** Phases in which AI is deliberately absent, to maintain skills.

**The tension.**

> **Deep competence vs. social efficiency.**
> Learning with friction is slow. The risk is a cognitive caste: elites with deep education, masses trained for passive use.

**Way out.** Measure depth by **quality of judgement**, not by time spent. Democratise access to assisted critical-thinking tools. Make offline periods a right, not a privilege.

## CHAPTER 4 — Design Ethics and Operational Principles (The Code)

**Principle.** Rights must be translated into binding, verifiable and — where possible — open-source technical requirements.

**Design requirements**

1. **Provenance and open-standard watermarking.** Verifiable marking for synthetic content, based on public standards (e.g. C2PA), to avoid monopolies of control.
2. **Training mode.** Gradual reduction of AI assistance to maintain human skills.
3. **Graceful degradation.** In case of failure, AI must return control to the human safely, not collapse silently.
4. **Verifiable plurality.** Educational stimuli must be auditable by independent third parties.

**The tension.**

> **Security vs. open innovation.**
> Strict requirements raise costs and may consolidate the monopoly of Big Tech. Open source is the countermeasure, but it is not enough: public computing, open data, oriented procurement are needed.

## CHAPTER 5 — The Charter of 5 Fundamental Rights (The Law)

1. **Right to Cognitive Sovereignty.** To know when one is interacting with an AI and to understand its basic logic.
2. **Right to Continuity of Autonomy.** Not to become dependent and unable to perform critical functions without AI.
3. **Right to Information Integrity.** To clearly distinguish documentary reality from synthetic simulation.
4. **Right to Non-Manipulation of Behaviour.** Protection from the exploitation of psychological vulnerabilities for commercial or political ends.
5. **Right to Equitable Access to Cognitive Infrastructures.** Basic AI as an essential public service for education and healthcare.

**The final tension.**

> **Universal rights vs. geopolitical realism.**
> Fragmentation risks creating incompatible "cognitive blocs", where truth itself becomes relative to jurisdiction.

## CHAPTER 6 — Political Economy and Governance *(new in v1.1)*

Rights without funding are wishes. We must address:

- **Who pays.** Digital public service, funding for computing, oriented public procurement.
- **Concentration.** Antitrust, interoperability, access obligations for foundation models.
- **Value chains.** Who owns data, weights, hardware, energy, distribution.
- **Taxation and rents.** Taxing cognitive rents, not innovation.
- **Global South.** Non-paternalistic access, data sovereignty, local capacity.

**Tension.** Regulating too early freezes arrangements; regulating too late consolidates them. There is no optimal point: there is continuous negotiation.

## CHAPTER 7 — Stress Test *(new in v1.1)*

A manifesto that does not withstand edge cases is decorative. Here are the cases it must be tested against:

| Scenario | Critical question | Right in tension |
|---|---|---|
| Electoral deepfake 48h before the vote | Who removes it? How fast? With what remedy? | Information Integrity |
| Autonomous agent managing finances and making a mistake | Who is liable for the damage? | Cognitive Sovereignty |
| Open-weight model without guardrails | Can it be banned? How? | Security vs. openness |
| Medical system suggesting diagnoses | Who signs the decision? | Continuity of Autonomy |
| Authoritarian state using AI for surveillance | What does the international community oppose? | Non-Manipulation |
| Students delegating every task | How is dependency measured? | Continuity of Autonomy |

If a right does not survive these tests, it must be rewritten.

## EPILOGUE — The Way of the Prism

AI is not a mirror that reflects passively, nor an oracle that reveals truth. It is a **prism** that breaks reality into many colours.

But beware: **not all colours are equal**. Some are legitimate perspectives; others are distortions, errors, manipulations. The prism does not authorise relativism. Distinguishing perspective from verifiable fact is the human task that no AI can replace.

The challenge is not to find the "right" colour. It is to learn to see the whole spectrum, recognise false colours, and consciously choose which to use, when, for what purpose, under whose responsibility.

> *Final tension note.* If every generation must renegotiate these principles, this Manifesto is an illusion of stability. Perhaps the only truly universal principle is **continuous negotiation**, with clear procedural rules. And that too is a tension.

**Cross-cutting mechanisms:** independent audits, regulatory sandboxes, public procurement, open standards, technical whistleblowing, funded investigative journalism.

## APPENDIX B — Minimum glossary

- **Autonomous agent:** AI system that plans and executes actions with margins of initiative.
- **Selective cognitive forgetting:** targeted deletion of memory or behavioural patterns.
- **Graceful degradation:** safe fallback returning control to the human.
- **Watermarking:** verifiable marking of synthetic content.
- **Layered transparency:** differentiated levels of explanation for different audiences.
- **Non-optimisation:** conscious renunciation of "better" solutions in the name of non-measurable values.
